A practical resource

Check readiness before
paying for an application.

Writing can clarify a well-defined program. It cannot make an ineligible organization eligible or create evidence that does not exist.

Wellspring Grants editorial resource · October 19, 2026

Use these questions to decide whether writing is your current bottleneck. They do not estimate the probability of an award. Every funder has its own requirements.

1. Do you have a specific opportunity?

Read the current funder guidelines and actual application. A foundation's general mission is not enough to establish that it accepts your type of organization, location, project or request. Check whether the opportunity is open, invitation-only or preceded by an inquiry.

If no opportunity is selected, start with research rather than paying for a written application. Our current writing service begins with the opportunity you bring.

2. Can you document eligibility?

Confirm status, geography, organization type, financial requirements and allowed uses against this funder. Pending status or fiscal sponsorship must be accepted by the specific program; do not assume all funders treat them the same way.

If the answer is unclear, ask the funder where appropriate or use a pre-purchase service-fit inquiry. A writing provider's screening does not replace the funder's determination.

3. Is the project defined?

Can your team describe what will happen, who it serves, where and when it runs, who does the work, and what the request pays for? If those decisions remain unresolved, finalize them before asking a writer to make the project sound finished.

4. Do you have honest evidence?

List actual past accomplishments, relevant staff experience and documented need. New organizations can distinguish planned work from completed work; they should not invent a track record.

For future outcomes, explain what you intend to measure and how. A goal is not a result. A proposed partnership is not a confirmed commitment.

5. Can the budget and attachments be prepared?

Identify the request amount, project costs and relevant financial period. Assign responsibility for the funder's budget form, financial statements, status documents, signatures and other attachments. Writing a budget narrative is different from preparing the underlying financial records.

6. Is there enough time to finish?

Work backward from the exact deadline and timezone. Include writing, your review, revisions if needed, internal approval, signatures and portal entry. A writing turnaround is not a submission guarantee.
