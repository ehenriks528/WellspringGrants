An annotated fictional demonstration. Read the prompts, responses and editorial notes before deciding whether the approach fits your team.

Fictional demonstration. Harbor Path Learning and Community Learning Fund are invented for this example. Every program number and budget amount is illustrative. This is not a client application, a funded grant or evidence of past service results.

This focused example contains four narrative responses. Your actual application follows the accepted scope and the funder's requirements; this demonstration is not a promise that every funder uses these prompts.

1. Describe the need.

Fictional prompt · Maximum 150 words

Harbor Path Learning proposes after-school reading support for 30 students at one community center. The program would create a regular setting for guided practice over 24 weekly sessions.

The application needs documented local evidence of reading-support needs before submission. No district results, family survey findings or access statistics were supplied for this demonstration. Those facts should be added from a verified source rather than inferred from the program plan.

69 words

Editorial note: This answer deliberately exposes an evidence gap. A convincing need statement should be grounded in real information; a fictional local statistic would make the example look stronger while teaching the wrong practice.

2. Explain the proposed project.

Fictional prompt · Maximum 200 words

Harbor Path Learning requests $12,000 to offer 24 weekly after-school reading sessions at one community center, with an enrollment goal of 30 students. Facilitator time, books and materials, and transport support make up the proposed request.

The program would combine regular attendance with guided reading practice. Attendance records would show how consistently students participate. A defined reading assessment before and after the program would help the team examine changes in participants' reading performance.

The final application needs the actual session schedule, staffing qualifications, participant recruitment plan and assessment method. These details must be confirmed before the proposal is submitted.

99 words

Editorial note: Activities, scale and budget connect to the same source brief. The answer distinguishes a planned program from proven results and names the remaining decisions.

3. State the outcomes and measurement.

Fictional prompt · Maximum 120 words

The program aims to enroll 30 students and have at least 24 complete 18 or more of the 24 weekly sessions. Attendance will be recorded for each session.

The team also plans to compare a defined reading assessment administered before and after participation. The assessment instrument, administration process and reading-improvement target need to be confirmed. Completion and assessment findings will be reported separately; attendance alone will not be treated as evidence of improved reading.

74 words

Editorial note: A measurable participation goal is different from a learning outcome. The answer avoids inventing a reading-gain percentage without a chosen measure or basis.

4. Explain the budget.

Fictional prompt · Maximum 120 words

The $12,000 request comprises $8,000 in facilitator costs, $2,000 for books and materials, and $2,000 in transport support. These amounts support the proposed 24-session reading program at one community center.

The final budget should specify the basis for each amount and any funder-required cost categories. No matching contribution or other donor commitment is assumed in this demonstration.

57 words

Editorial note: The narrative matches the budget below. A final application would need the underlying rates or quotes, rather than invented calculations.

What remains before this could be submitted?

  • Verified evidence of need and a suitable real funder.
  • Actual eligibility, prompts, limits and deadline requirements.
  • Confirmed staffing, recruitment, assessment and cost assumptions.
  • Required attachments, internal approval and a complete final fact check.

A good handoff makes unresolved facts visible. It does not turn missing information into a polished claim. Use the application checklist to prepare your own source information.
